River floods

Every day the 51-member GloFAS v4 ensemble forecasts river discharge 30 days ahead on a ~5 km river network. Each site sits on the modelled river nearest the town that carries a real share of the local flow.

Thresholds are the 2-, 5- and 20-year floods of the same model's own daily discharge (), from a Gumbel fit (L-moments) to water-year (Oct–Sep) maxima. Comparing a model with its own climate removes its bias, which is how GloFAS itself issues flood signals, so flows are shown as multiples of the 2-year flood. When GloFAS v5 goes live, its thresholds are built automatically and the site switches over.

Flash-flood rain: seven models, wet ground and snowmelt

Cities with a flash-flood history are tracked through rainfall from seven global ensembles combined into one forecast, each model counting equally however many members it has:

Rain is summed over Iran local days and 3-day windows and compared with the 2-, 5- and 20-year 1-day and 3-day rainfall of ERA5 (). A second check follows the LISFLOOD model behind GloFAS: rain plus degree-day snowmelt, times the share of ground wet enough to shed it, against return levels of the same quantity from ERA5. It can raise a warning, never lower one, and lowers the rain needed by at most 30%. Rain alerts count days 1–.

Levels, people at risk and feeds

People at risk are residents within 5 km of a river site's town or 10 km of a city centre (JRC Global Human Settlement Layer, 2025). Every alert lists them with what to do, in English and Persian.

Alerts are also published as CAP 1.2 messages (the international Common Alerting Protocol) and an RSS feed. The page checks for new model runs four times an hour. Below large dams the real flow depends on release decisions; those sites are marked. Not an official warning: always follow the Iran Meteorological Organization and local crisis-management authorities.
